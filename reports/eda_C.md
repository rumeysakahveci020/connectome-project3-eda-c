# Workstream C: neurotransmitters and local circuits

FlyWire FAFB 783 (female) and male CNS v1.0 (male, brain part). EDA looks at structure and labels only; population and threshold are stated per line.

- **C1 (central and optic neurons, share of neurons)**: acetylcholine dominates in both datasets. Central: 52% in FAFB and 55% in the male CNS by predictedNt (68% by consensusNt, where the male CNS dopamine falls from 10% to 1%: it is Kenyon cells mislabelled); GABA 12% / 12%, glutamate 18% / 16%, unknown 1% / 6%. Optic: acetylcholine 66% / 59%, GABA 15% / 10%, glutamate 18% / 19%, unknown 0.2% / 10%, plus 2% histamine in the male CNS only. The FAFB classifier has no histamine class, so its photoreceptors are labelled acetylcholine or glutamate; weighted by output synapses it is 60% and 58% acetylcholine; so the female-male gap in neuron share largely disappears when weighted and comes mostly from labels, not from biology (not tested).
- **C2 (all neurons)**: median confidence is 0.69 in FAFB and 0.94 in the male CNS, so there is no common cutoff (0.6 removes 34% vs 2%). FAFB: acetylcholine 0.72 most and octopamine 0.42 least confident; male CNS: acetylcholine 0.96 most and serotonin 0.60 least confident. Against nt_known, FAFB nt_type agrees for 87%, but 10.5% of known cholinergic neurons are predicted dopamine and 16.5% of known glutamate neurons GABA.
- **C2b (male CNS)**: predictedNt calls 99.9% of Kenyon-type neurons dopamine, consensusNt says acetylcholine (88% agreement overall).
- **C3 (FAFB)**: a hemilineage is a family of neurons born from the same progenitor lineage, so its neurons should share a transmitter. 78% of 183 hemilineages have at least 90% of one fast transmitter, as expected (Eckstein et al. 2020). The 25 below 80% are mixed or poorly predicted; they include the catch-all labels putative_primary and primary, which are not true hemilineages (full list in the notebook).
- **C4 (FAFB central, tau = 10)**: making glutamate excitatory shifts the median net input by 0.26 to 1.03 in 9 of the 12 largest neuropils and flips its sign in 5 (FB, GNG, IPS, SMP, SPS); MB_CA does not change.
- **C5 (mushroom body, tau = 10)**: FAFB has 5,293 neurons, 20,645 edges and 117 isolated neurons, median in- and out-degree 1 and 3 (male CNS: 4,435, 30,314, 48); FAFB edges fall from 473,647 (tau = 1) to 42,326 (tau = 5) to 20,645 (tau = 10). At tau = 5 FAFB has 38 isolated, against 195 and 35 in week 2 (the week 2 cell-type selection now gives 138 and 32). The subgraph is thin at tau = 10; whether it is dense enough to train on is a modelling question I did not test.
- **C6 (FAFB mushroom body, tau = 10, 100 null graphs)**: reciprocal pairs are 2.0 times the null (4,950 vs 2,420); reciprocal pair, 201, 120D, 210, 300 are over-represented and 021D, 021U, 021C, 111D, 111U, 030T, 030C, 120C under-represented (|z| up to 1,639 because a few hubs dominate).

**What surprised me:** Changing how the male CNS region is counted (upstream + downstream, the group's rule, instead of pre + post) moved the mushroom body subgraph from 4,468 to 4,435 neurons and from 12 to 48 isolated neurons at tau = 10; I am looking into which neurons moved.
