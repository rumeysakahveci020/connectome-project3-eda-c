# Workstream C: neurotransmitters and local circuits

FlyWire FAFB 783 (female) and male CNS v1.0 (male, brain part). EDA looks at structure and labels only; population and threshold are stated per line.

- **C1 (central and optic neurons, share of neurons)**: acetylcholine dominates in both datasets. Central, by nt_type_corrected: 66% in FAFB and 65% in the male CNS (by nt_type before the correction: 53% and 56%; consensusNt gives 68% for the male CNS); GABA 12% / 12%, glutamate 17% / 16%, unknown 0% / 5%. Optic: acetylcholine 60% / 59%, GABA 13% / 10%, glutamate 17% / 19%, unknown 0.1% / 10%, plus 2% histamine in the male CNS only. The FAFB classifier has no histamine class, so its photoreceptors are labelled acetylcholine or glutamate; weighted by output synapses it is 58% and 58% acetylcholine; so the female-male gap in neuron share largely disappears when weighted and comes mostly from labels, not from biology (not tested).
- **C2 (all neurons)**: median confidence is 0.69 in FAFB and 0.94 in the male CNS, so there is no common cutoff (0.6 removes 34% vs 2%). FAFB: acetylcholine 0.72 most and octopamine 0.42 least confident; male CNS: acetylcholine 0.96 most and serotonin 0.60 least confident. Against nt_known, FAFB nt_type agrees for 87%, but 10.5% of known cholinergic neurons are predicted dopamine and 16.5% of known glutamate neurons GABA.
- **C2b (male CNS)**: predictedNt calls 99.9% of Kenyon-type neurons dopamine, consensusNt says acetylcholine (88% agreement overall); the correction applies the Kenyon-cell override only.
- **C3 (FAFB)**: a hemilineage is a family of neurons born from the same progenitor lineage, so its neurons should share a transmitter. 78% of 187 hemilineages have at least 90% of one fast transmitter, as expected (Eckstein et al. 2020), counted on nt_type_corrected. The 25 below 80% are mixed or poorly predicted; they include the catch-all labels putative_primary and primary, which are not true hemilineages (full list in the notebook).
- **C4 (FAFB central, tau = 10, nt_type_corrected)**: making glutamate excitatory shifts the median net input by 0.25 to 1.15 in 9 of the 12 largest neuropils and flips its sign in 7 (FB, GNG, IPS, PLP, PVLP, SMP, SPS); MB_CA does not change.
- **C5 (mushroom body, tau = 10)**: FAFB has 5,258 neurons, 18,653 edges and 145 isolated neurons, median in- and out-degree 1 and 2 (male CNS: 4,435, 30,314, 48); FAFB edges fall from 448,478 (tau = 1) to 36,570 (tau = 5) to 18,653 (tau = 10). At tau = 5 FAFB has 55 isolated, against 195 and 35 in week 2 (the week 2 cell-type selection now gives 138 and 32). The subgraph is thin at tau = 10; whether it is dense enough to train on is a modelling question I did not test.
- **C5b (mushroom body types, nt_type_corrected)**: types with at least 5 neurons and no transmitter above 90%: FAFB 0 of 22 (0 neurons), MaleCNS 1 of 24 (12 neurons); they need a look before they get one sign.
- **C6 (FAFB mushroom body, tau = 10, 100 null graphs)**: reciprocal pairs are 2.0 times the null (4,884 vs 2,415); reciprocal pair, 201, 210, 300 are over-represented and 021D, 021U, 021C, 111D, 111U, 030T, 030C, 120C under-represented (|z| up to 1,958 because a few hubs dominate).

**What surprised me:** how much the correction changed the comparison between the datasets: central acetylcholine went from 53% to 66% in FlyWire and from 56% to 65% in the male CNS, so the gap between them went from 3 to 1 points.
